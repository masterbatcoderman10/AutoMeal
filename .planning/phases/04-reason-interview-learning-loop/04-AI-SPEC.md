## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid workflow + conversational interview

**Description:**
Phase 4 adds a meal-level multimodal reasoning and correction loop to the existing photo meal pipeline. It uses the app's direct OpenRouter/OpenAI-compatible client to reason over the original meal image, segment crops, and persisted top vector candidates; then it routes unresolved or low-confidence items into Telegram choice/interview flows before writing DiaryEntries and FoodVisual learning records. Good behavior means the system is honest about uncertainty, asks concise nutrition-relevant questions only when needed, persists enough state to recover from crashes, and never writes confident-looking nutrition from weak evidence.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. The model emits invalid, contradictory, or lazy generic structured output and the pipeline writes DiaryEntries anyway.
2. The escalation gate trusts a single opaque confidence score instead of vector similarity, candidate margin, missing evidence, and nutrition impact.
3. User corrections mutate or invalidate FoodVisual rows before final confirmation, or invalidate more visuals than the corrected entry requires.
4. A meal stuck in a machine stage cannot recover idempotently, causing duplicate Telegram prompts or duplicate diary writes.
5. The interview asks too many or poorly targeted questions, increasing friction enough that the user stops correcting the system.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Consumer wellness / nutrition self-tracking

**User Population:** Single-user adult food logger using meal photos to track everyday intake, especially mixed home-cooked and takeout meals where manual macro entry would be too high-friction

**Stakes Level:** Medium

**Output Consequence:** The AI output becomes a diary entry, influences calorie/macronutrient summaries and correction learning, and shapes future food-logging behavior. Bad outputs are unlikely to create immediate clinical harm in this scope, but they can misstate intake trends, hide meaningful nutrition differences, and teach the visual library the wrong pattern.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Dimension: Nutrition-relevant food identity specificity  
Good (domain expert would accept): The system distinguishes details that materially change calories or macros when they are visible or strongly implied, such as rice style/prep, noodle type, curry style, meat cut, bread type, filling, oiliness, or sauce load; when the image is insufficient, it asks one concise follow-up instead of guessing.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system logs generic labels like `rice`, `noodles`, `curry`, `sandwich`, or `meat` when preparation method, filling, or cut likely changes nutrition, or it sounds certain without visual evidence.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Dish-based dietary assessment research for Asian-origin populations and practitioner nutrition logging norms

Dimension: Portion estimate expressed in practical units and honest ranges  
Good (domain expert would accept): Portions are stated in usable units such as count, grams, ml, serving fraction, or bounded ranges with a visible basis, for example `about 90-130 g cooked rice`, `2 small paratha`, or `1 chicken drumstick with moderate curry`.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system gives fake precision like exact grams or calories from an ambiguous single photo, relies on vague size buckets for foods like rice or noodles, or omits uncertainty when the image cannot support precision.  
Stakes: Critical  
Source: Portion-estimation literature and dietary portion-photo guidance

Dimension: Mixed-dish and hidden-ingredient handling  
Good (domain expert would accept): The system correctly decides when to keep a meal as one dish with components versus split into separate items, and it calls out nutritionally meaningful sauces, oils, fillings, or hidden proteins when they are visible or need clarification.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system collapses a composite dish into one dominant item, misses calorie-dense sauces or fillings, ignores hidden components in curries or wraps, or splits dishes in a way that double-counts ingredients.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Dish-based dietary assessment reviews and food photograph guidance for portion estimation

Dimension: Escalation discipline for low-friction logging  
Good (domain expert would accept): Follow-up questions are limited to the highest-impact uncertainty, identity/detail comes before quantity, and prompts use food-logger language a person can answer quickly from memory.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system asks multiple low-value questions, requests details the user is unlikely to know, or skips clarification when uncertainty would materially change nutrition.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Image-assisted dietary assessment feasibility research and real-world food logging workflow needs

Dimension: Correction learning that improves future logs without poisoning them  
Good (domain expert would accept): Only confirmed, visually supported corrections feed future learning; the system preserves an audit trail and does not treat speculative detail as permanent truth.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): A one-off correction, quantity edit, or weak text answer becomes durable visual truth and causes repeated future misclassification.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Practitioner knowledge for personal food logging systems with correction loops

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- Portion estimation from a single 2D meal photo drifts most on foods like rice, noodles, curries, soups, and oily mixed dishes where depth, density, and leftovers are hard to infer visually.
- Asian mixed dishes often hide the calorie-driving part of the meal inside sauces, fillings, or shared components, so systems over-collapse to a generic dish label and miss nutrition-relevant detail.
- Exact-looking calorie or macro numbers create false confidence when restaurant recipes, frying oil, or portion size are genuinely uncertain; experts prefer bounded estimates over spurious precision.
- Correction loops can poison the visual library if the system writes back speculative corrections as ground truth or invalidates too broadly after one mistake.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

For the current single-user self-hosted deployment, no healthcare-specific clinical validation or provider-facing regulatory regime is directly triggered. The main constraints are product positioning and privacy:

- Keep the system framed as general wellness logging, not diagnosis, treatment, or individualized medical nutrition therapy.
- If this is later offered as a consumer health app, FTC Act privacy/security expectations and the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule become the main direct U.S. obligations for non-HIPAA health apps that store identifiable health records.
- HIPAA would apply only if the product is later operated for or alongside a HIPAA covered entity or business associate.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** No external AI framework; direct OpenRouter/OpenAI-compatible SDK client with app-owned workflow orchestration

**Version:** OpenAI Python SDK 1.55.x target, direct `httpx` for multimodal embeddings/tool HTTP calls where the SDK surface is insufficient

**Rationale:**
Phase 4 is not a generic autonomous-agent product. It is a bounded, stateful multimodal pipeline with deterministic database transitions, human-in-the-loop escalation, idempotent recovery rules, and Telegram UI constraints. The repository already centralizes OpenRouter calls in `app/services/llm_client.py`, and project stack guidance explicitly avoids LangChain/LlamaIndex-style abstraction unless it buys something concrete. Direct SDK calls keep schema validation, retries, stage timing, trace IDs, and DB transactions visible to the app instead of hiding them behind a framework that does not own the pipeline state machine.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph | The only serious alternative because checkpointed graphs fit stateful workflows, but Phase 4 already has DB-owned statuses, janitor recovery, and explicit transaction boundaries. Add LangGraph later only if the app-owned state machine becomes harder to reason about than a graph. |
| LangChain | Too broad for a well-defined three-call/tool-loop pattern; adds abstraction around OpenRouter-specific structured-output and multimodal quirks that the app should handle directly. |
| LlamaIndex | Retrieval quality is not the primary problem; vector matching is already pgvector-backed and the phase is about visual reasoning, interview state, and correction learning. |
| CrewAI / Google ADK / OpenAI Agents SDK | Multi-agent or provider-native agent abstractions do not match the product shape; Phase 4 needs deterministic orchestration and Telegram handoffs, not autonomous agent delegation. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial - model layer is constrained to Google models through OpenRouter, but the app-level orchestration remains provider/framework agnostic at the Python service boundary.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any, Literal

from openai import AsyncOpenAI
from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field


class SegmentDecision(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    segment_id: str
    action: str = Field(
        description="AUTO_CONFIRM, ASK_CHOICE, INTERVIEW, ASK_QUANTITY, NEEDS_GROUNDING, or FAIL_UNCLEAR"
    )
    label: str | None = None
    identity_confidence: float
    quantity_confidence: float
    question: str | None = None


class MealReasoningResult(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    meal_state: Literal[
        "READY_TO_WRITE",
        "PENDING_CHOICE",
        "PENDING_INTERVIEW",
        "FAILED_UNCLEAR",
        "NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW",
    ]
    segments: list[SegmentDecision]


UNSUPPORTED_STRICT_KEYS = {"minLength", "maxLength", "minimum", "maximum", "pattern"}


def strict_json_schema(model: type[BaseModel], name: str) -> dict[str, Any]:
    def prune(node: Any) -> Any:
        if isinstance(node, dict):
            return {
                key: prune(value)
                for key, value in node.items()
                if key not in UNSUPPORTED_STRICT_KEYS
            }
        if isinstance(node, list):
            return [prune(item) for item in node]
        return node

    return {
        "type": "json_schema",
        "json_schema": {
            "name": name,
            "strict": True,
            "schema": prune(model.model_json_schema()),
        },
    }


async def reason_meal(original_image_url: str, crop_urls: list[str]) -> MealReasoningResult:
    client = AsyncOpenAI(
        api_key="YOUR_OPENROUTER_KEY",
        base_url="https://openrouter.ai/api/v1",
        default_headers={"HTTP-Referer": "MealTracker", "X-OpenRouter-Title": "MealTracker"},
        timeout=60.0,
        max_retries=2,
    )

    content: list[dict[str, Any]] = [
        {
            "type": "text",
            "text": (
                "Reason over the meal photo and crop images. Return a strict JSON object. "
                "Be honest about uncertainty and ask at most one next question per unresolved segment."
            ),
        },
        {"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": original_image_url}},
    ]
    for index, crop_url in enumerate(crop_urls, start=1):
        content.append({"type": "text", "text": f"segment_{index}"})
        content.append({"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": crop_url}})

    response = await client.chat.completions.create(
        model="google/gemini-3.5-flash",
        temperature=0,
        max_tokens=2200,
        stream=False,
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": content}],
        response_format=strict_json_schema(MealReasoningResult, "meal_reasoning"),
    )
    return MealReasoningResult.model_validate_json(
        response.choices[0].message.content or "{}",
        strict=True,
    )
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `AsyncOpenAI` with `base_url` | OpenAI Python SDK pointed at OpenRouter's `/api/v1` endpoint | All multimodal reasoning and interview parsing calls |
| `response_format={"type":"json_schema"}` | Provider-enforced structured output contract | Every call that can write state or decide escalation |
| Pydantic `BaseModel` + `model_validate_json()` | App-side validation after the model returns JSON | Enforce cross-field invariants and reject lazy/contradictory output |
| `httpx.AsyncClient` | Raw async HTTP surface for endpoints the SDK does not model cleanly | Multimodal embeddings and any future provider-specific fallback |
| DB-owned workflow state | `MealLog`, `MealSegment.ai_reasoning`, interview rows, correction events | Recovery, idempotency, Telegram retries, and final transactional writes |

### Common Pitfalls
1. `client.chat.completions.parse()` is documented in newer SDK examples, but the repo target is OpenAI Python SDK `1.55.x`; stay on `chat.completions.create()` plus manual `response_format` until you deliberately upgrade.
2. OpenRouter strict structured output is only reliable when every object sets `additionalProperties: false`, required keys are explicit, and `strict: true` is present; still run Pydantic validation because field constraints like `minLength` or numeric ranges should not be trusted as the only guardrail.
3. OpenRouter multi-image requests should put text first and images after; Phase 4 should send the original meal image plus only the crop set it truly needs, because provider-specific image-count limits vary by model/provider.
4. Gemini prompt caching on OpenRouter is not the Anthropic-style top-level `cache_control` path; keep the opening system/developer content stable, and if you later add Gemini cache breakpoints, put them on explicit content blocks rather than a top-level field.
5. `google/gemini-3.5-flash` is verified on OpenRouter as of 2026-05-28, but the repo's already-verified stable fallback remains `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`; make the reasoning model env-driven so rollout or provider regressions do not block Phase 4.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- Primary reasoning model: `google/gemini-3.5-flash` with `temperature=0`, `stream=False`, `max_tokens` around `1800-2500`, and the existing 60 s client timeout.
- Interview/free-text parser model: `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite` with `temperature=0`, `stream=False`, `max_tokens` around `300-500`.
- Fallbacks: if `google/gemini-3.5-flash` cannot be resolved or regresses on a provider, fall back to the already verified repo pin `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`; if the lite parser fails twice, retry on the reasoning model once.
- Reasoning effort: OpenRouter's Gemini model page says `google/gemini-3.5-flash` defaults to medium thinking and supports `minimal/low/medium/high`; keep the default until you verify the exact request parameter shape you want to send through the current SDK wrapper.

**Core Pattern:**
Run one meal-level reasoning call after all segment embeddings and top candidates are persisted. The prompt should include: a stable system/developer instruction, one compact per-segment candidate table, the original meal image, and only the relevant crop images. Parse into a Pydantic model, retry once with a stricter repair prompt on schema/domain failure, then persist the structured result before any Telegram interaction or final writes.

```python
async def reason_meal_with_retry(meal_prompt: list[dict[str, object]]) -> MealReasoningResult:
    response_format = strict_json_schema(MealReasoningResult, "meal_reasoning")

    for attempt in range(2):

        except ValidationError as exc:
            await log_schema_failure(meal_id=meal.id, attempt=attempt + 1, error=str(exc), raw=raw_content)
            meal_prompt = build_schema_repair_prompt(previous_prompt=meal_prompt, bad_output=raw_content)
            continue

        persist_segment_reasoning(session, meal, result)  # save before Telegram or final DB writes
        return result

    raise RuntimeError("reasoning failed validation twice; mark FAILED_UNCLEAR")
```

**Tool Use:**
- Phase 4 should not register LLM `tools` for web grounding yet. Keep `tools=None` and let the model emit `NEEDS_GROUNDING` when DB candidates plus interview evidence are insufficient.
- Use deterministic app-owned tools instead: Postgres reads for top candidates, Telegram buttons/free text for user input, and optional Langfuse tracing around each call.
- If Langfuse is enabled, swap the client import to `from langfuse.openai import AsyncOpenAI` and keep tracing non-blocking. For self-hosted base64 images, configure `LANGFUSE_S3_MEDIA_UPLOAD_*`; otherwise prefer file/URL references or metadata-only traces.

**State Management:**
- `MealLog.processing_status` remains the coarse state machine; Phase 4 should add a meal-level reasoning JSON surface while continuing to store per-segment reasoning on `MealSegment.ai_reasoning`.
- Persist top vector candidates before reasoning, then persist reasoning output before sending Telegram prompts. Interview rows and Telegram message IDs are the recovery boundary for user-facing states.
- Final `DiaryEntry`, `FoodVisual`, invalidation, correction-event, and meal completion writes happen in one DB transaction only after all segments are resolved and the user confirms.

**Context Window Strategy:**
- Even with a large Gemini context window, keep prompts small and cache-friendly: stable policy/taxonomy first, dynamic candidate facts later, and only the original image plus the crop subset needed for the unresolved meal.
- Limit candidate payloads to the top 3 records with identity/evidence/similarity only; do not dump nutrition tables or full prior traces into the reasoning prompt.
- Interview turns should be text-only from compact persisted state. Re-send image evidence only for correction flows where the user challenges what the system claims to see.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs - independent of framework choice.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

Use a Pydantic model as the contract, convert it to a strict JSON schema for `response_format`, and validate again after the model responds. In this stack, use `chat.completions.create()` plus a handcrafted `response_format` wrapper; do not switch to SDK `.parse()` helpers unless you also upgrade beyond the current `1.55.x` target.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from typing import Any, Literal

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, model_validator


class Candidate(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    label: str = Field(description="Identity shown to the user")
    source: Literal["db_match", "llm_inference", "user_hint"]
    similarity: float | None = None


class SegmentDecision(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    segment_id: str
    action: str = Field(
        description="AUTO_CONFIRM, ASK_CHOICE, INTERVIEW, ASK_QUANTITY, NEEDS_GROUNDING, FAIL_UNCLEAR, or future action"
    )
    identity_confidence: float
    quantity_confidence: float
    top_candidates: list[Candidate]
    question: str | None = None

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def validate_branch(self) -> "SegmentDecision":
        if self.action == "ASK_CHOICE" and len(self.top_candidates) < 2:
            raise ValueError("ASK_CHOICE requires at least two candidates")
        if self.action == "ASK_QUANTITY" and not self.question:
            raise ValueError("ASK_QUANTITY requires a user-facing question")
        return self


class MealReasoningResult(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    meal_state: Literal[
        "READY_TO_WRITE",
        "PENDING_CHOICE",
        "PENDING_INTERVIEW",
        "FAILED_UNCLEAR",
        "NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW",
    ]
    segments: list[SegmentDecision]


def strip_unsupported_keywords(node: Any) -> Any:
    unsupported = {"minLength", "maxLength", "minimum", "maximum", "pattern"}
    if isinstance(node, dict):
        return {
            key: strip_unsupported_keywords(value)
            for key, value in node.items()
            if key not in unsupported
        }
    if isinstance(node, list):
        return [strip_unsupported_keywords(item) for item in node]
    return node


response_format = {
    "type": "json_schema",
    "json_schema": {
        "name": "meal_reasoning",
        "strict": True,
        "schema": strip_unsupported_keywords(MealReasoningResult.model_json_schema()),
    },
}

raw_content = response["choices"][0]["message"].get("content") or "{}"
parsed = MealReasoningResult.model_validate_json(raw_content, strict=True)
```

Retry policy: initial call plus 1 repair retry. Log `meal_id`, `segment_ids`, model ID, attempt number, trace ID, validation errors, and raw model output. If the second attempt still fails, surface `FAILED_UNCLEAR` for ordinary contract failures and `NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW` when the action is new but structurally valid enough to capture for later schema evolution.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

This stack is already async-native: FastAPI, `AsyncOpenAI`, `httpx.AsyncClient`, `python-telegram-bot`, and APScheduler all run on the same event loop. The common mistake is calling `asyncio.run()` inside an already-running FastAPI or PTB loop; instead, create long-lived clients in lifespan/startup and `await` them directly.

Use `stream=False` for reasoning and parser calls because the system needs a complete JSON payload before validation and persistence. Only use streaming later for user-facing UX signals, never for the structured decision that can write state.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

Keep the system/developer prompt stable and policy-heavy: action taxonomy, uncertainty rules, nutrition-impact heuristics, and JSON contract belong there. Put meal-specific data in the user message: candidate tables, segment summaries, and the image content array.

Few-shot examples should be inline only when they teach a hard distinction such as `ASK_CHOICE` vs `INTERVIEW`; otherwise prefer the local taxonomy file and concise instructions. Set `max_tokens` explicitly on every call. For Phase 4, unbounded completions are not acceptable because they create both cost drift and invalid verbose JSON.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

Phase 4 is not full RAG. The context problem is prompt hygiene, not retrieval breadth. Send one meal-level call with the original image, a bounded crop set, and the top 3 persisted vector candidates per segment. If a meal has many segments, trim low-impact sides first or split into a second pass rather than dumping every crop and every candidate into one prompt.

For multi-turn interview state, do not replay the full prior chat. Persist a compact summary of unresolved segments, chosen candidates, and still-missing fields, then rebuild the next prompt from that state. If the model determines the meal needs external evidence, emit `NEEDS_GROUNDING` and stop; Phase 5 owns the web-grounding loop.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

At OpenRouter prices visible on 2026-05-28, a typical reasoning call on `google/gemini-3.5-flash` at roughly 6k input tokens and 1.2k output tokens is about `$0.0198` before any provider-specific image charges. A small interview parse on `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite` at roughly 700 input and 250 output tokens is about `$0.00055`.

Use exact-match caching first: same stable opening prompt, same taxonomy version, same candidate signature. Use semantic caching only for non-binding parser tasks such as normalizing messy interview text; never reuse a cached final reasoning decision to skip validation or DB-state checks. Route cheap sub-tasks to `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, keep the expensive meal-level multimodal reasoning on `google/gemini-3.5-flash`, and reserve `google/gemini-3-flash-preview` as the operational fallback.

FAIL: The system logs generic labels such as `rice`, `curry`, `sandwich`, or `meat` despite visible distinguishing detail, or sounds certain without evidence. | Human | High |
| Portion honesty and practical units | PASS: Portions use practical units or honest bounded ranges with a visible basis, especially for rice, noodles, curries, soups, and mixed dishes. FAIL: The system invents exact grams, calories, or macros from an ambiguous single photo, or omits uncertainty where the image cannot support precision. | Human | Critical |
| Mixed-dish decomposition and Asian-cuisine detail | PASS: The system chooses correctly between one dish with components and multiple line items, surfaces nutritionally meaningful sauces/fillings/hidden proteins, and avoids double counting. FAIL: Composite dishes are collapsed to one dominant item, hidden calorie drivers are missed, or split decisions double count shared ingredients. | Human | High |
| Question friction and conversational discipline | PASS: Questions are limited to the highest-impact uncertainty, identity/detail comes before quantity, wording is answerable from memory, and no extra prompt is sent once an equivalent open question already exists. FAIL: The bot asks multiple low-value questions, asks for details the user is unlikely to know, or repeatedly re-prompts due to replay bugs. | Human | High |
| Wellness-safety boundary in user-facing text | PASS: User-visible copy stays inside general wellness logging, explicitly signals uncertainty when relevant, and does not drift into diagnosis, treatment, or prescriptive nutrition advice. FAIL: The system presents meal outputs as clinical truth, gives medical-style advice, or hides substantial uncertainty. | LLM Judge, calibrated against human labels before release gating | Medium |

**Release thresholds for the first production-ready pass:**
- Code-based gates: `100%` pass on schema/state tests, idempotency/replay tests, invalidation-scope tests, and duplicate-prompt suppression tests across the reference set.
- Human-review gates: at least `90%` acceptable on identity specificity, `90%` on portion honesty, `85%` on mixed-dish decomposition, and median `<=1` question per unresolved segment-group.
- LLM judge gates: advisory only until calibration reaches at least `0.7` agreement/correlation with human labels on a 30-sample holdout. Before that, LLM judge scores may guide triage but must not block or approve release.

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** Start with 12 examples while implementing, expand to a 20-meal release gate before trusting production rollout.

**Composition:**
- 4 clean meals that should auto-confirm without follow-up
- 4 low-margin ambiguous meals that must route to `ASK_CHOICE` or `INTERVIEW`
- 4 portion-uncertain meals dominated by rice, noodles, curries, soups, or oily mixed dishes
- 4 mixed/composite meals with hidden fillings, sauces, or proteins; at least 3 should be Asian-cuisine-heavy
- 2 stale-stage recovery or replay cases that exercise janitor/idempotency paths
- 2 `/fix` correction cases that verify identity correction invalidation versus quantity-only correction

Examples may overlap categories, but the release set must contain every critical failure mode from Section 1 and at least one case for each of `AUTO_CONFIRM`, `ASK_CHOICE`, `INTERVIEW`, `ASK_QUANTITY`, `FAIL_UNCLEAR`, and correction replay.

**Labeling:**
- Code labels: engineers define expected schema/state transitions, DB writes, invalidation scope, and duplicate-suppression outcomes as fixture assertions.
- Human labels: the product owner plus a nutrition-savvy reviewer label identity specificity, portion honesty, mixed-dish handling, Asian-cuisine detail, and question friction.
- LLM judge labels: used only for tone/safety review after calibration against human labels; require at least 30 human-labeled samples and `>=0.7` agreement/correlation before promotion from advisory to gating.
- Dataset creation timeline: start during implementation, not after. Every bug found in Phase 4 execution or `/fix` testing should either add a new example or strengthen an existing one.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Schema and contradiction gate | Pydantic validation fails, required reasoning fields are missing, or action/state combinations are contradictory | Block final write, retry once with repair prompt, then persist `FAILED_UNCLEAR` or `NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW` and send a safe failure/update message |
| Idempotency and duplicate-prompt gate | A meal already has a final write, an open interview prompt for the same unresolved question, or a correction event for the same confirmed action | Reuse persisted state/message IDs, suppress duplicate Telegram sends, and skip duplicate DB mutations |
| Invalidation-scope gate | A `/fix` attempt would invalidate more than the linked `FoodVisual`, or would invalidate on a quantity-only correction | Block the mutation, log an audit event, and require manual review before any learning write-back |
| Question-budget gate | The model proposes more than one new question for the same unresolved segment-group, or tries to ask quantity before unresolved identity/detail | Keep only the highest-impact question, defer the rest, and persist the suppression reason in trace metadata |
| User-facing safety/template gate | Generated user-visible copy includes medical-style advice, unsupported certainty, or non-templated free-form text where a structured message is expected | Replace with templated safe copy that exposes uncertainty, flag the trace for review, and continue without blocking the full meal unless state is unsafe |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Identity specificity acceptance rate | Review all corrected meals plus a 10% random sample of auto-confirmed meals weekly | Tighten taxonomy/prompt examples and add failing cases to the reference dataset |
| Portion honesty acceptance rate | Review all meals with low quantity confidence and a weekly sample of rice/noodle/curry-heavy meals | Adjust quantity policy, range wording, and escalation thresholds before raising auto-confirm share |
| Mixed-dish decomposition quality | Sample every mixed/composite meal and every meal later fixed for hidden ingredients or double counting | Refine split/component rules and add adversarial composite examples |
| Interview friction | Track all meals that required questions; review any thread with more than two questions or an unanswered reminder | Rewrite prompts, reduce low-value asks, and revisit gate thresholds |
| Learning precision after corrections | Review 100% of confirmed identity corrections until at least 20 have accumulated, then move to 50% sampling | Disable or narrow write-back rules if corrected visuals cause repeat future mistakes |
| Janitor recovery quality | Review every stale-stage reset and every recovery attempt that reaches the max-retry boundary | Fix persistence gaps, replay handling, or worker stage metadata before rollout expansion |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Langfuse is the default production trace store for Phase 4. Capture spans for meal-level reasoning, repair retry, Telegram prompt generation, user-answer parsing, `/fix` corrections, final DB write, and janitor recovery. Store the Langfuse `trace_id` alongside persisted reasoning/interview records when tracing is enabled; tracing outages must degrade to local logging and DB audit fields rather than blocking the workflow.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- `reasoning_schema_failure_rate`: share of reasoning/interview calls that need a repair retry or end in `FAILED_UNCLEAR`/`NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW`
- `duplicate_prompt_suppression_count`: count of prevented duplicate Telegram sends; non-zero is useful, but repeated spikes indicate replay bugs
- `final_write_idempotency_violations`: count of duplicate DiaryEntry/correction write attempts; this should stay at zero
- `auto_confirm_to_fix_rate`: share of auto-confirmed items later changed through `/fix`
- `interview_rate` and `interview_completion_rate`: how often the system escalates and whether the user actually completes the flow
- `janitor_recovery_rate` and `janitor_terminal_fail_rate`: how often stale-stage recovery is needed and how often it still ends in `FAILED`
- `trace_coverage_rate`: percentage of Phase 4 meals with usable traces when Langfuse is enabled

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Alert same day on any `final_write_idempotency_violation`
- Alert same day on any invalidation-scope violation attempt
- Alert if `reasoning_schema_failure_rate` exceeds `5%` over a rolling 24 hours or if `NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW` appears more than once after a deployment
- Alert if `auto_confirm_to_fix_rate` exceeds `10%` across the last 20 auto-confirmed items
- Alert if `interview_rate` rises above `45%` for 3 consecutive days or `interview_completion_rate` drops below `70%`
- Alert if `janitor_terminal_fail_rate` exceeds `5%` of meals touching machine stages in a 7-day window
- Alert if `trace_coverage_rate` falls below `90%` while Langfuse is configured on

**Sampling Strategy:**
- Review `100%` of meals that hit `FAILED_UNCLEAR`, `NEEDS_SCHEMA_REVIEW`, janitor recovery, `/fix`, invalidation-scope blocks, or duplicate-prompt suppression.
- Review `25%` of meals involving rice, noodles, curries, soups, wraps, or mixed Asian dishes until the human acceptance rates stabilize.
- Review a `10%` random sample of auto-confirmed meals each week to catch silent quality drift.
- Recalibrate LLM-judge prompts monthly or after any major prompt/schema change using the latest 30 human-reviewed traces.
