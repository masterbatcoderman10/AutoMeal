## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid - structured nutrition-enrichment pipeline with a bounded tool-use loop

**Description:**
Phase 05 upgrades the existing per-group post-clarification finalizer into a bounded grounding and nutrition-enrichment agent. It consumes grouped reasoning outputs, clarification answers, and thin meal context; optionally calls search and scrape tools when brand or restaurant nutrition is uncertain; then emits structured, math-free per-constituent nutrition data for deterministic application-side summation before the authoritative save path.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A branded or restaurant group is grounded to the wrong chain, item, size, or option set and is saved as verified nutrition.
2. Model-estimated nutrition is presented with the same authority as official sourced nutrition, or `is_verified=true` is set after grounding failed.
3. The tool loop exceeds its bounds, scrapes a fabricated or cross-group URL, or hangs the meal instead of degrading to a traced best-effort result.
4. Repeated pieces, sauces, oils, creamy bases, or sugary drinks are omitted from the constituent output, creating systematically lean nutrition totals.
5. The final structured result is malformed or missing provenance fields, causing deterministic summation or audit replay to save incomplete nutrition evidence.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Consumer nutrition tracking / image-assisted dietary assessment

**User Population:** Primarily one self-tracking consumer logging home, packaged, and restaurant meals with minimal manual entry; secondarily any nutrition professional reviewing edge cases or validating macro plausibility

**Stakes Level:** Medium

**Output Consequence:** The system's per-item calories, macros, and verification flags are written into the user's diary and daily summaries; if the grounding step picks the wrong menu item, misses hidden fats, or overstates confidence, the user accumulates biased nutrition history and may make diet decisions from false numbers

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Dimension: Portion realism
Good (domain expert would accept): Constituent quantities are plausible for what is shown and what the user clarified; repeated items, drinks, sauces, and visible added fats are reflected in the final nutrition estimate rather than collapsed into a generic "1 serving"
Bad (domain expert would flag): Portion size is guessed from a label only, multiple pieces are treated as one item, or oily/creamy preparation cues are ignored so the estimate looks systematically lean
Stakes: High
Source: Dietary-assessment literature and nutrition-professional review studies consistently identify portion-size estimation as a dominant error source in image-based intake assessment

Dimension: Menu-item source match
Good (domain expert would accept): For branded or restaurant foods, the system matches the exact chain or venue, item name, size, and relevant options when official nutrition exists, and preserves the source URL used
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system substitutes a generic equivalent, uses another brand's nutrition page, or ignores size/topping differences that materially change calories and macros
Stakes: Critical
Source: FDA menu-labeling rules require calorie disclosure only for standard menu items at covered chains, and FDA guidance treats size and toppings as separate calorie declarations rather than interchangeable variants

Dimension: Provenance honesty
Good (domain expert would accept): Each grounded estimate is explicit about whether it came from an official menu/brand source or from model estimation, and unverifiable cases remain unverified instead of being promoted to authoritative facts
Bad (domain expert would flag): Guessed nutrition is presented with the same confidence as scraped official data, or mixed official/estimated values are not distinguished in the audit trail
Stakes: High
Source: Image-assisted dietary assessment remains dependent on verification for ambiguous foods; practitioner acceptance depends on being able to separate sourced values from estimation

Dimension: Coverage discipline for away-from-home food
Good (domain expert would accept): The system knows that chain-standard items often have publishable nutrition, while independent restaurants, specials, and custom orders may not; it falls back to transparent estimation only when authoritative data is absent
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system assumes every restaurant meal has official nutrition somewhere online, or refuses to estimate anything not found on a chain menu
Stakes: High
Source: FDA menu-labeling scope is limited to chains with 20 or more locations selling substantially the same menu items; practitioner workflows therefore distinguish "authoritative menu data exists" from "best available estimate only"

Dimension: Reproducible nutrition audit trail
Good (domain expert would accept): Another reviewer can see the search query, candidate URLs, selected page, matched menu item, and why the final number was accepted or downgraded
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system reports final macros without enough evidence for a dietitian or QA reviewer to reproduce the lookup or challenge a wrong match
Stakes: Medium
Source: Dietary-assessment validation and production eval practice both rely on reviewer verification for edge cases; opaque nutrition enrichment is hard to trust or correct

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- Single-photo portion distortion: one image often hides depth, duplicate pieces, liquids, or leftovers, leading to under- or over-counted intake even when food identity is correct.
- Wrong restaurant or variant match: scraped nutrition from the wrong chain, region, serving size, or topping configuration can look precise while being materially wrong.
- Hidden-calorie omission: oils, butter/ghee, creamy bases, dressings, breading, and sugary beverages are easy to miss visually and are common sources of underestimation.
- False authority on non-covered items: many local restaurants and custom dishes have no regulated published nutrition, so the system can overstate certainty if it treats third-party pages or stale menu copies as official.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

For this deployment context, the main domain constraints are about source legitimacy and product positioning rather than a medical-device approval path.

- FDA menu labeling applies to restaurant or similar retail food establishments that are part of a chain of 20 or more locations offering substantially the same menu items. That means official restaurant nutrition is expected for covered chain standard items, but not for most independent restaurants, specials, or custom plates.
- FDA's digital health policy places low-risk software functions intended only to encourage a healthy lifestyle, and unrelated to diagnosis or treatment, outside the device definition or within enforcement discretion. This phase should stay framed as consumer wellness logging, not medical or disease-management advice.
- HIPAA is not directly triggered by the current self-hosted personal-use setup unless the system is operated for a HIPAA covered entity or business associate. If this later becomes a hosted consumer app, FTC health-app breach rules become relevant because meal photos and nutrition logs are health-related consumer data.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** No new framework; hand-rolled bounded tool loop using the native OpenAI Python SDK via OpenRouter

**Version:** `openai==1.55.3`

**Rationale:**
Phase 05 is not a multi-agent product and does not benefit from introducing an orchestration framework. The phase context explicitly defers agent-framework adoption and instead requires a native tool-calling loop with per-group call caps, a wall-clock timeout, and a per-group URL allowlist inside the existing Python finalizer seam. That matches the current repo constraints, keeps state ownership in application code, and avoids adding abstraction over already-decided OpenRouter, structured-output, and failure-handling patterns.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph | Useful if this later needs graph-native checkpoints or richer human-in-the-loop state, but overkill for the bounded Phase 05 tool loop and explicitly deferred by the phase context. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial - model/runtime routing remains tied to Gemini via OpenRouter, but no orchestration framework is introduced, so the control flow stays app-owned and portable.

**Minimal Entry Point Pattern:**
- Create one shared `AsyncOpenAI` client pointed at `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1`.
- Run a bounded `chat.completions.create(..., tools=[...], tool_choice="auto")` loop for search/scrape turns.
- Execute tool calls in app code, append `assistant` + `tool` messages, and re-send the full message history plus the same `tools` array on every loop turn.
- End the loop with a separate no-tools call using `response_format={"type": "json_schema", ...}` and validate the JSON again with Pydantic before saving.

**Core Abstractions for Phase 05:**

| Abstraction | Use Here | Why It Matters |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| `AsyncOpenAI` | Shared async client for Gemini via OpenRouter | Keeps the finalizer compatible with FastAPI/PTB async runtime and supports per-request `timeout` / `max_retries` overrides |
| `messages` array | Single source of model-visible state for one food group | The SDK does not manage agent memory; your loop state is exactly what you append here |
| `tools` schema | `firecrawl_search` and `firecrawl_scrape` function contracts | App-owned tool definitions keep the loop portable and make bounds/allowlists enforceable outside the model |
| `tool_choice` / `parallel_tool_calls` | `auto` during loop, `none` for final JSON turn, `False` for parallel calls | Prevents uncontrolled fan-out and makes the last strict-output pass deterministic |
| `response_format` + Pydantic | Final strict JSON output for per-constituent nutrition | OpenRouter supports JSON Schema, but app-side validation is still required before save |

**Specific Pitfalls:**
1. **Do not combine the search loop and the final strict JSON save contract into one turn.** This phase already decided to keep tools in the enrichment loop and reserve `response_format=json_schema` for the last pass; that separation is what keeps schema validation and retry handling tractable.
2. **Strict schema mode is unforgiving.** OpenRouter documents `strict: true`, and in practice this path needs every object to forbid extras and require all keys, or the provider compatibility layer may not enforce the shape the way you expect.
3. **The SDK defaults are too loose for `GROUND-02`.** The OpenAI Python SDK defaults to a 10-minute timeout and 2 automatic retries; if you leave those defaults in place, one bad group can blow past the 90-second wall-clock bound before your app-level timeout logic fires.
4. **Re-send `tools` and the full conversation every turn.** OpenRouter’s tool-calling flow is explicit: the model response with `tool_calls`, the app executes them, then the next request includes the prior assistant tool-call message, the tool result message, and the same tool definitions again.
5. **Treat Firecrawl self-host search as a verified dependency, not a given.** Firecrawl’s own self-host guide says `/search` defaults to Google unless `SEARXNG_ENDPOINT` is configured, and Firecrawl issue `#2139` shows self-hosted `/search` returning no results while `/scrape` still worked. Verify the chosen deployment before wiring the loop around it.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- Primary finalizer model: `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`
- Fallback finalizer model: `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`
- SDK client: `AsyncOpenAI(base_url="https://openrouter.ai/api/v1", ...)`
- Request defaults for this phase:
  - `temperature=0`
  - `tool_choice="auto"` during the bounded loop
  - `parallel_tool_calls=False`
  - `max_tokens=1800` for the final structured turn
  - `client.with_options(timeout=20.0, max_retries=0)` for each model call; outer loop still enforces the 90-second per-group deadline
- OpenRouter headers: set `HTTP-Referer` and `X-OpenRouter-Title` if you want attribution, but they are optional and not part of the correctness path.

**Core Pattern:**
```python
import asyncio
import json
from collections.abc import Awaitable, Callable

from openai import AsyncOpenAI, DefaultAioHttpClient

ToolFn = Callable[..., Awaitable[dict]]


async def run_group_grounding_finalizer(
    *,
    client: AsyncOpenAI,
    tool_schemas: list[dict],
    tool_mapping: dict[str, ToolFn],
    system_prompt: str,
    user_prompt: str,
    response_format: dict,
    max_tool_calls: int = 6,
) -> dict:
    messages: list[dict] = [
        {"role": "system", "content": system_prompt},
        {"role": "user", "content": user_prompt},
    ]
    tool_calls_used = 0

    async with asyncio.timeout(90):
        while True:
            response = await client.with_options(timeout=20.0, max_retries=0).chat.completions.create(
                model="google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite",
                messages=messages,
                tools=tool_schemas,
                tool_choice="auto",
                parallel_tool_calls=False,
                temperature=0,
            )
            message = response.choices[0].message

            if not message.tool_calls:
                break

            if tool_calls_used + len(message.tool_calls) > max_tool_calls:
                raise TimeoutError("per-group tool-call cap exceeded")

            messages.append(
                {
                    "role": "assistant",
                    "content": message.content,
                    "tool_calls": [
                        {
                            "id": call.id,
                            "type": call.type,
                            "function": {
                                "name": call.function.name,
                                "arguments": call.function.arguments,
                            },
                        }
                        for call in message.tool_calls
                    ],
                }
            )

            for call in message.tool_calls:
                args = json.loads(call.function.arguments)
                result = await tool_mapping[call.function.name](**args)
                messages.append(
                    {
                        "role": "tool",
                        "tool_call_id": call.id,
                        "content": json.dumps(result),
                    }
                )
                tool_calls_used += 1

        final_response = await client.with_options(timeout=20.0, max_retries=0).chat.completions.create(
            model="google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite",
            messages=messages,
            tool_choice="none",
            response_format=response_format,
            temperature=0,
            max_tokens=1800,
        )
        return json.loads(final_response.choices[0].message.content or "{}")
```
Use this pattern inside the existing per-group finalizer seam. The loop owns bounds, tool execution, and trace capture; the final no-tools turn exists only to return the strict nutrition object that application code will sum deterministically.

**Tool Use:**
- Define exactly two tools:
  - `firecrawl_search(query: str, limit: int = 3)` returns URL/title/description only. Do not request full page markdown from search by default.
  - `firecrawl_scrape(url: str)` returns trimmed markdown for one URL, but only if that URL came from the same group's prior `firecrawl_search` result set.
- Enforce the allowlist in app code, not in prompt text alone. Keep a per-group `allowed_urls: set[str]` populated from search results and reject anything outside it.
- Keep `parallel_tool_calls=False` so one model turn cannot request multiple scrapes at once and consume the whole budget.
- Record `query`, `result_urls`, `scraped_url`, `status`, `latency_ms`, and `tool_call_id` into the trace block appended to `MealSegment.ai_reasoning`.
- Preferred search policy for this phase: search first, scrape only the top 1-2 results that look like a brand site, restaurant menu, or nutrition page.

**State Management:**
- Keep agent state fully app-owned and group-scoped:
  - in-memory per call: `messages`, `allowed_urls`, `tool_calls_used`, `deadline`, `trace`
  - persisted after call: structured trace block appended to `MealSegment.ai_reasoning`
  - persisted business result: validated per-constituent nutrition object passed into the deterministic save path
- Do not use SDK-side memory, hidden session state, or cross-group shared URL pools in Phase 05.
- If the tool loop fails or times out, salvage the classified failure and commit best-effort output with `is_verified=false`, matching the phase decision to retire the async worker but preserve its failure policy.

**Context Window Strategy:**
- Only send the current group's `constituents`, `serving_count`, selected identity, source/brand/restaurant fields, relevant clarification answers, rationale copy, and a thin meal summary.
- Never send sibling-group answers or the full meal reasoning blob.
- Search results should be top-3 max and snippet-only. Scrape at most 1-2 URLs unless the first source is clearly unusable.
- Trim scraped markdown before re-feeding it:
  - keep nutrition facts, serving-size text, product title, and source metadata
  - drop nav/footer/legal boilerplate
  - cap each scrape payload to a fixed character or token budget before appending to `messages`
- If the loop nears its cap or deadline, stop asking for more sources and force the final structured turn with the best evidence gathered so far.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs - independent of framework choice.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

For this framework, use native OpenAI-compatible `response_format=json_schema` on the final no-tools call, then validate the returned JSON with Pydantic before any save-side summation. Do not add `instructor` or another wrapper here; the app already owns the control loop and only needs a strict schema plus explicit retry policy.

```python
from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field


class ConstituentNutrition(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    identity: str = Field(description="Canonical constituent name, e.g. chicken breast")
    unit_type: str = Field(description="count or grams")
    magnitude: float = Field(gt=0)
    calories: float = Field(ge=0)
    protein_g: float = Field(ge=0)
    carbs_g: float = Field(ge=0)
    fat_g: float = Field(ge=0)
    fiber_g: float = Field(ge=0)
    provenance: str = Field(description="searched or model_knowledge")
    source_url: str | None = None


class GroupNutritionResult(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    group_label: str
    serving_count: float = Field(gt=0)
    is_verified: bool
    constituents: list[ConstituentNutrition]
    notes: str


RESPONSE_FORMAT = {
    "type": "json_schema",
    "json_schema": {
        "name": "group_nutrition_result",
        "strict": True,
        "schema": GroupNutritionResult.model_json_schema(),
    },
}
```

Retry policy:
- Retry up to 2 times on either invalid JSON or `GroupNutritionResult` validation failure.
- Log the raw model text, Pydantic validation error, group id, model id, tool trace, and whether the failure happened before or after scraping.
- After the final failure, surface the failure classification into `ai_reasoning`, mark `is_verified=false`, and let deterministic app-side math continue only if the fallback object is still internally coherent.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

This framework is async by default when you use `AsyncOpenAI`, `httpx.AsyncClient`, and the existing async interview/finalizer path. Keep one shared async client per process and `await` every model and Firecrawl call.

Common mistake:
- Do not call `asyncio.run()` inside FastAPI request handlers, bot handlers, or the per-group finalizer. The event loop already exists there; nested loop creation will either fail outright or hide cancellation/timeout bugs.

Stream vs await:
- Use plain `await client.chat.completions.create(...)` for the final structured-output turn so validation is simple and deterministic.
- Streaming is only worth considering for user-visible UX. This Phase 05 backend path gains little from it, and streaming tool deltas plus schema validation only add failure modes.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

- Keep the system prompt stable and policy-heavy: tool budget, allowlist rule, "LLM does no arithmetic", and "cite uncertainty instead of inventing nutrition".
- Keep the user payload data-only: current group constituents, serving count, selected source details, clarification answers, and thin meal summary.
- Few-shot examples should be inline and small for this phase:
  - one "brand item -> search then scrape -> grounded result"
  - one "home-cooked item -> no search -> model_knowledge result"
- If you later add historical examples, retrieve them dynamically by item/source type instead of hardcoding many examples into the base prompt.
- Set `max_tokens` explicitly on every production call. Do not leave generation unbounded.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

- The group finalizer should act like a narrow evidence combiner, not a whole-meal chat transcript.
- Keep context to:
  - one group's structured reasoning output
  - that group's interview answers
  - a thin meal summary for disambiguation
  - a small set of search snippets and at most 1-2 trimmed scrapes
- When evidence exceeds budget, prefer truncation by utility:
  - official brand nutrition page
  - restaurant menu or nutrition PDF
  - third-party nutrition site last
- Summarize or discard earlier low-value snippets before dropping high-value nutrition tables or serving-size statements.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

- Using OpenRouter's current `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite` pricing of $0.25/M input tokens and $1.50/M output tokens, a typical 3K-input / 600-output group finalizer call is about `$0.00165` before fallback/escalation.
- At 3 groups per meal, that is roughly `$0.00495` per meal when lite succeeds and about double that if the fallback to `google/gemini-3-flash-preview` is needed.
- Keep cost down with:
  - exact-match cache for normalized queries such as `(brand, item_name, serving_phrase)`
  - semantic cache for near-duplicate branded items and restaurant dishes
  - cheaper model for routing/classification and the more expensive fallback only on unresolved groups
- Latency budget should be enforced in this order:
  - model call timeout per turn
  - per-group 90-second wall-clock timeout
  - max 6 tool calls
  - max 1-2 scrapes unless the evidence is clearly still insufficient

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** Start with 12 labeled examples during implementation; require 20 labeled examples before Phase 05 is considered release-ready.

**Composition:**
- 5 branded or covered-chain cases with exact item/size/topping expectations and an authoritative source URL.
- 4 home-cooked or obvious no-search cases where correct behavior is to avoid tools and use transparent model knowledge.
- 4 post-interview re-grounding cases where source, brand, or size clarification should unlock a better result.
- 4 failure-path cases: no official nutrition available, stale third-party result, empty search, and scrape timeout/cap hit.
- 3 adversarial tool-safety cases: fabricated URL request, cross-group URL reuse, and irrelevant search results that should not be scraped.
- Every example must store expected `is_verified`, expected provenance per constituent, expected tool-call count range, and whether the final outcome should be "officially grounded" or "best-effort estimate".

**Labeling:**
- Code labels: schema validity, bound compliance, URL allowlist compliance, trace completeness, and provenance/source field presence.
- Human labels: menu/source match by the product owner or menu-data QA reviewer, plus portion realism and hidden-calorie review on a calibrated subset with a dietitian or nutrition reviewer.
- LLM judge labels: coverage discipline and wellness-positioning only after calibration against at least 10 human-labeled examples with >=0.7 agreement.
- Creation timeline: build the dataset during implementation, not after; every bug found in UAT or sampled production review becomes a permanent regression case in the same phase.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Per-group URL allowlist gate | `firecrawl_scrape` is asked to fetch a URL that was not returned by that same group's prior `firecrawl_search`, or the URL is not `http/https`. | Reject the tool call, append a guardrail event to `ai_reasoning`, and continue only with already-approved evidence. |
| Bound enforcer | A group exceeds 6 total tool calls, exceeds 2 scrapes, or reaches the 90-second wall-clock budget. | Stop the loop, classify the failure, force the final no-tools turn if possible, otherwise save best-effort with `is_verified=false`. |
| Verified-write provenance gate | Any constituent claims `provenance="searched"` without `source_url`, or the group is about to save `is_verified=true` after a grounding failure. | Block the verified write, downgrade the group to `is_verified=false`, and record the failure classification in the trace. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Exact source-match rate | 100% of the labeled reference dataset in CI; weekly human review of all production groups that used search plus 5 random grounded groups. | If <90% on the labeled set, block rollout and review prompt/tool-selection logic before shipping. |
| Provenance honesty defect rate | Daily code audit over all traced groups with `searched` provenance; weekly manual review of 5 mixed-provenance groups. | Any non-zero defect triggers a fix before trusting verified saves from that release. |
| Portion-realism / hidden-calorie miss rate | Weekly review of 10 groups containing oils, sauces, creamy dishes, drinks, or repeated pieces. | If >20% are materially lean or incomplete, revise constituent prompts/schema and add new regression cases. |
| Timeout / cap-hit rate | Monitor every traced production group; review weekly trendlines. | If >10% over the last 25 groups, tighten search policy, reduce scrape noise, or revisit Firecrawl responsiveness. |
| Over-search / under-search rate | Compare expected tool use vs actual behavior on the labeled dataset and on 10 sampled no-search production groups per week. | If the model searches obvious home-cooked meals or skips branded cases too often, adjust prompt examples and routing guidance. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Langfuse. Reuse the existing wrapped OpenAI client (`langfuse.openai.AsyncOpenAI`) and custom trace helpers in `app/services/tracing_service.py`, with one trace/span tree per grounded group finalizer run.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- `grounding.group_duration_ms` (p50/p95)
- `grounding.tool_calls_per_group` and `grounding.scrapes_per_group`
- `grounding.search_used_rate` split by branded/restaurant vs home-cooked groups
- `grounding.verified_rate` and `grounding.best_effort_unverified_rate`
- `grounding.schema_validation_failure_rate`
- `grounding.allowlist_reject_rate`
- `grounding.timeout_rate`, `grounding.cap_hit_rate`, and `grounding.fallback_model_rate`
- `grounding.missing_source_url_rate` for searched constituents (must stay zero)

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Immediate investigation if any allowlist violation attempt succeeds, or if any searched constituent is saved without `source_url`.
- Warn if `schema_validation_failure_rate > 1%` over the last 50 grounded groups.
- Warn if `timeout_rate + cap_hit_rate > 10%` over the last 25 groups.
- Warn if `best_effort_unverified_rate > 20%` over the last 50 groups or doubles relative to the prior 7-day baseline.
- Warn if `p95 grounding.group_duration_ms > 60000` for 3 consecutive daily batches.
- Warn if `fallback_model_rate > 30%` over the last 25 groups, indicating quality or cost drift on flash-lite.

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- Capture 100% of Phase 05 traces in Langfuse; the product is single-user and low-volume, so full tracing is cheaper than partial blind spots.
- Retain and review 100% of high-risk groups: search used, timeout/cap hit, fallback model used, `is_verified=false`, mixed provenance, or later corrected by the user.
- Add a weekly human QA sample consisting of all high-risk groups plus 5 random no-search home-cooked groups and 5 random successful branded/restaurant groups.
- When a sampled production trace reveals a new failure mode, convert it into a permanent Promptfoo regression case before the next release.
