## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Extraction

**Description:**
Phase 2 is a deterministic multimodal extraction pipeline for a single-user meal-tracking product. An accepted uploaded photo is classified as food vs. not-food, segmented into distinct visible food regions, cropped to disk, labeled in plain language, and summarized in a terse Telegram sentence. "Good" means the system skips clear non-food inputs, produces only valid grouped food regions, avoids duplicate counting, and emits a human-readable result that matches what the user would intuitively say is on the plate.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A clear non-food image is misclassified as food and produces downstream segments or user-visible meal output.
2. The segmentation stage accepts invalid, hallucinated, or duplicated bounding boxes and saves bad crops or double-counts the same food.
3. The system over-segments food into atomic pieces instead of distinct visible food regions, making the result sentence noisy or misleading.
4. Crop persistence writes the wrong image region or wrong file path, breaking downstream matching and later auditability.
5. The final human-readable label sentence confidently names foods that do not match the visible meal.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Consumer wellness / personal nutrition logging

**User Population:** A single self-tracking consumer using meal photos to reduce food-logging friction; secondary evaluators are nutrition-tracking practitioners who judge whether the meal was segmented and named the way a human would reasonably log it.

**Stakes Level:** Medium

**Output Consequence:** If Phase 2 extraction is wrong, later phases start from the wrong crops and wrong food names, which degrades matching, nutrition lookup, and user trust. The output is not used for diagnosis, treatment, or acute clinical decisions.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

Dimension: Plate-level grouping that matches how a person would log the meal  
Good (domain expert would accept): One curry bowl, one bread stack, and one side dish are each captured once as distinct visible food regions. Separate servings of the same food are split only when they are clearly in different places.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system counts every chicken piece or bread triangle separately, merges two distinct dishes into one crop, or double-counts the same dish through overlapping boxes.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Phase 2 decisions `D-05` to `D-08`; dietary-assessment research consistently treats mixed-dish segmentation as a core validity problem.

Dimension: Visible-food naming with appropriate specificity  
Good (domain expert would accept): Labels match what a knowledgeable tracker would actually enter from the photo, such as `chicken curry`, `pita bread`, or `mixed vegetables`; uncertain items stay broad or hedged instead of pretending to know hidden details.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): The system claims unseen ingredients, brand names, or precise cooking styles that are not visually supported, or uses labels so vague that the meal is not meaningfully identifiable.  
Stakes: High  
Source: CVPR 2025 VLM dietary-assessment evaluation highlights difficulty with fine-grained cooking-style and visually similar food recognition; Phase 2 scope requires plain-language labels from visible evidence only.

Dimension: Robustness to ordinary meal-photo conditions  
Good (domain expert would accept): Results stay usable under normal phone-photo variation such as indoor lighting, shadows, slight camera angle changes, and non-food clutter around the plate, as long as the meal is clearly present.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): Slight lighting or angle changes cause obvious foods to disappear, extra segments to appear, or a clear meal photo to be rejected as non-food.  
Stakes: High  
Source: 2026 narrative review of AI dietary assessment reports meaningful degradation from lighting, camera angle, shadowing, and occlusion in free-living settings.

Dimension: Mixed-dish and cuisine-aware handling  
Good (domain expert would accept): Curries, stews, stir-fries, and other visually integrated dishes are grouped and labeled at the dish level, and culturally specific meals are not collapsed into a nearest Western analogue.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): Mixed dishes are fragmented into unsupported ingredients, or non-Western foods are renamed as generic lookalikes because the system lacks cuisine coverage.  
Stakes: High  
Source: Nutrients 2024 app-validation study and the 2026 narrative review both identify mixed dishes and culturally diverse foods as persistent weak spots.

Dimension: Suppression of low-signal extras  
Good (domain expert would accept): Tiny garnish, trace sauces, and negligible extras are ignored unless they are substantial enough to count as a meaningful side or component the user would actually log.  
Bad (domain expert would flag): Parsley sprigs, tiny dips, or incidental sauces become separate reported items, making the result sentence noisy and reducing trust.  
Stakes: Medium  
Source: Phase 2 decision `D-08`; nutrition-app validation literature notes current systems already struggle with condiments, oils, and sauces, so over-reporting them creates false precision.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- Mixed dishes such as curries, stews, casseroles, and rice bowls get either over-split into imagined ingredients or merged into an unhelpfully generic blob.
- Lighting, shadows, steep camera angles, bowls, and self-occlusion hide boundaries, causing missed foods, duplicate regions, or incorrect NOT_FOOD decisions.
- Culturally diverse meals are mislabeled as visually similar Western defaults because common food-image datasets underrepresent many regional cuisines.
- Sauces, oils, condiments, and ingredients hidden inside the dish are either omitted entirely or guessed with false confidence, which later propagates into nutrition error.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No healthcare-specific regulation appears to attach to the current deployment shape: this is a self-hosted, single-user consumer wellness logger, not a clinical service. HIPAA is generally not implicated unless the system is operated for or on behalf of a covered entity. FDA's January 2026 general wellness guidance indicates low-risk software intended to encourage a healthy lifestyle, without diagnosis or treatment claims, is outside the medical-device category. If this later becomes a consumer-distributed app or starts syncing identifiable health data from multiple sources, FTC health-privacy and Health Breach Notification Rule obligations become directly relevant.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** OpenAI Python SDK via the existing `OpenRouterClient` service abstraction

**Version:** `openai==1.55.3`

**Rationale:**
Phase 2 is a narrow, linear extraction workflow rather than a framework-heavy agent system. The repository already has a working OpenRouter integration surface in [app/services/llm_client.py](/Users/mali/Documents/Projects/MealTracker/app/services/llm_client.py:1), the project constraints explicitly route chat/vision calls through the OpenAI SDK with `base_url` swapped to OpenRouter, and adding LangChain-, LlamaIndex-, or agent-style orchestration here would add surface area without reducing the real risks in this phase: detect accuracy, bounding-box validity, retry handling, crop persistence, and user-facing label correctness.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph 1.2.1 | Strong fit for later durable workflows, but overkill for this phase's direct detect -> segment -> label path and would introduce state-graph scaffolding before it buys reliability value. |
| Google ADK 2.x | Better fit for teams leaning into native Gemini and Google tooling, but current project constraints are OpenRouter-first and already implemented around the OpenAI SDK shape. |
| LangChain | Broad ecosystem, but adds abstraction overhead for a simple structured-output extraction slice that the existing service layer can already express directly. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — provider-specific model pins and OpenRouter compatibility are accepted, but the implementation intentionally stays at the direct SDK/service layer instead of coupling Phase 2 to a higher-level orchestration framework.

### Entry Point Pattern
```python
import base64
from pathlib import Path

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field

from app.services.llm_client import get_llm_client


class DetectFoodResult(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    is_food: bool
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)
    reason: str


def build_response_format(model: type[BaseModel], name: str) -> dict:
    schema = model.model_json_schema()
    schema["additionalProperties"] = False
    return {
        "type": "json_schema",
        "json_schema": {
            "name": name,
            "strict": True,
            "schema": schema,
        },
    }


def jpeg_to_data_url(image_path: Path) -> str:
    encoded = base64.b64encode(image_path.read_bytes()).decode("utf-8")
    return f"data:image/jpeg;base64,{encoded}"


async def detect_food(image_path: Path) -> DetectFoodResult:
    llm = get_llm_client()
    response = await llm.chat_completion(
        model="google/gemma-4-31b-it",
        messages=[
            {
                "role": "system",
                "content": (
                    "You are the first gate in a meal-logging pipeline. "
                    "Return JSON only. Be conservative: if unsure, set is_food=false."
                ),
            },
            {
                "role": "user",
                "content": [
                    {"type": "text", "text": "Classify this image as food or not-food."},
                    {"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": jpeg_to_data_url(image_path)}},
                ],
            },
        ],
        response_format=build_response_format(DetectFoodResult, "detect_food_result"),
    )
    content = response["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]
    return DetectFoodResult.model_validate_json(content)
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `OpenRouterClient` | Repo-local wrapper around `AsyncOpenAI` plus a raw `httpx.AsyncClient` for embeddings. | Always. Phase 2 should extend this wrapper if it needs extra request params rather than creating a second LLM client. |
| `chat.completions.create(...)` | The direct async OpenAI-compatible call already used by `OpenRouterClient.chat_completion()`. | Detect, segment, and label calls in Phase 2 all stay on this path. |
| Multimodal `messages` content arrays | `{"type": "text"}` plus `{"type": "image_url"}` parts inside one user message. | Every detect, segment, and crop-label request that includes an image. |
| `response_format={"type": "json_schema", ...}` | Structured-output contract enforced by the model provider for compatible models. | Detect and segment must use this; labeling should also use it if the output feeds persistence or rendering. |
| Pydantic post-validation | Local validation after the API call, using repo-owned models and validators. | Every structured response before writing `MealSegment` rows or changing `MealLog.processing_status`. |

### Common Pitfalls
1. `OpenRouterClient.chat_completion()` currently exposes only `model`, `messages`, `response_format`, and `tools`. Phase 2 needs explicit `temperature`, `max_tokens`, and optionally `extra_body`, so widen the wrapper once instead of bypassing it in service code.
2. Do not mix `client.chat.completions.parse()` and the repo's current `.create()` wrapper in the same phase. The SDK helper is valid, but this repo currently returns `model_dump()` dictionaries; keeping one request path makes retries, logging, and test fixtures much simpler.
3. OpenRouter structured outputs are model-dependent. If a fallback provider or model without `response_format` support is selected, the request can fail outright; keep Phase 2 model IDs pinned and avoid loose routing for detect/segment.
4. For local files, the image part must be a full data URL such as `data:image/jpeg;base64,...`, not raw base64 bytes. Also keep the text prompt before the `image_url` part; OpenRouter explicitly recommends that ordering.
5. Streaming is the wrong default for this phase. Detect and segment both need fully valid JSON before any DB write, so `await` the full response and validate it before changing status or saving crops.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- Detect: `google/gemma-4-31b-it`, `temperature=0`, `max_tokens=120`, structured output required, no tools.
- Segment primary: `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`, `temperature=0`, `max_tokens=700`, structured output required, no tools.
- Segment retry: `google/gemini-3.5-flash`, same schema and token budget, used only after invalid JSON, invalid boxes, duplicate-heavy output, or empty usable results.
- Label: `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`, `temperature=0.1`, `max_tokens=160`, structured output preferred so the final Telegram sentence is rendered from typed labels instead of free-form prose.
- Wrapper change required before implementation: extend `OpenRouterClient.chat_completion()` to accept pass-through kwargs such as `temperature`, `max_tokens`, and later `extra_body`; keep the repo on one shared client surface.

**Core Pattern:**
Use one async service function per stage, each with:
1. A small Pydantic schema that matches exactly what the stage is allowed to return.
2. A `response_format` built from that schema.
3. A single multimodal user message containing text first, image second.
4. Local validation before any DB write.
5. A bounded retry policy that upgrades the segmentation model once instead of endlessly re-prompting.

```python
import base64
from pathlib import Path
from typing import Any

from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, field_validator

from app.services.llm_client import get_llm_client


class SegmentCandidate(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    label: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=80)
    box_2d: list[int]
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)

    @field_validator("box_2d")
    @classmethod
    def validate_box(cls, box: list[int]) -> list[int]:
        if len(box) != 4:
            raise ValueError("box_2d must contain exactly four integers")
        y0, x0, y1, x1 = box
        if not all(0 <= value <= 1000 for value in box):
            raise ValueError("box_2d values must stay in 0..1000")
        if y0 >= y1 or x0 >= x1:
            raise ValueError("box_2d must be ordered as [y0, x0, y1, x1]")
        return box


class SegmentResult(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    segments: list[SegmentCandidate]


def build_response_format(model: type[BaseModel], name: str) -> dict[str, Any]:
    schema = model.model_json_schema()
    schema["additionalProperties"] = False
    return {
        "type": "json_schema",
        "json_schema": {
            "name": name,
            "strict": True,
            "schema": schema,
        },
    }


def image_to_data_url(image_path: Path) -> str:
    encoded = base64.b64encode(image_path.read_bytes()).decode("utf-8")
    return f"data:image/jpeg;base64,{encoded}"


def extract_message_text(response: dict[str, Any]) -> str:
    content = response["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]
    if isinstance(content, str):
        return content
    if isinstance(content, list):
        return "".join(part.get("text", "") for part in content if part.get("type") == "text")
    raise TypeError("Unexpected chat completion content format")


async def segment_photo(image_path: Path) -> SegmentResult:
    llm = get_llm_client()
    response_format = build_response_format(SegmentResult, "segment_result")
    prompt = (
        "Return distinct visible food regions only. Group foods at the dish/container level. "
        "Ignore tiny garnish and minor sauces. Return JSON only."
    )

    for attempt, model in enumerate(
        ["google/gemini-3-flash-preview", "google/gemini-3.5-flash"],
        start=1,
    ):
        response = await llm.chat_completion(
            model=model,
            messages=[
                {"role": "system", "content": prompt},
                {
                    "role": "user",
                    "content": [
                        {"type": "text", "text": "Segment this meal photo."},
                        {"type": "image_url", "image_url": {"url": image_to_data_url(image_path)}},
                    ],
                },
            ],
            response_format=response_format,
        )
        try:
            return SegmentResult.model_validate_json(extract_message_text(response))
        except Exception:
            if attempt == 2:
                raise

    raise RuntimeError("unreachable")
```

**Tool Use:**
Phase 2 should not enable model-side tools. Pass `tools=None` for detect, segment, and label calls. SearXNG and Firecrawl are later-phase reasoning tools and would only increase latency, cost, and failure surface here. Keep the `tools` parameter in `OpenRouterClient` intact for later phases, but do not add tool loops to the vision slice.

**State Management:**
- Persist orchestration on the existing `MealLog.processing_status` enum: `PENDING -> DETECTING -> SEGMENTING -> COMPLETED`, with `FAILED` for unusable detect/segment outcomes after bounded retries.
- Do not introduce a new `LABELING` status in Phase 2 unless there is a concrete operational need; with the current enum, label generation belongs inside the `SEGMENTING` window before the final completion write.
- Persist only validated boxes to `MealSegment.bounding_box`, then write crops to `/data/uploads/crops/{segment_id}.jpg` and store the path in `MealSegment.cropped_image_url`.
- Treat a partially invalid segmentation payload as a full-stage failure for that attempt. This matches Phase 2 decision `D-09` and prevents mixed-validity writes.
- Build the Telegram sentence from stored `MealSegment.label` values after all rows for the meal are committed, then collapse duplicate labels in the renderer instead of in the model prompt.

**Context Window Strategy:**
- Keep each call single-purpose and single-image. Detect sees the original photo only; segment sees the original photo only; label sees either one accepted crop at a time or a very small crop batch with explicit indexes.
- Do not pass prior bot messages, prior meal history, or previous raw JSON back into the model unless the retry path specifically needs a terse error note such as "your last boxes were invalid because coordinates were reversed."
- When a retry is needed, resend the same image with the same schema and a tighter system instruction, but change the model on the second attempt instead of piling on more transcript.
- Set `max_tokens` explicitly per stage so a malformed segmentation attempt cannot consume an unbounded output budget.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

```python
from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, Field, field_validator


class MealSegmentCandidate(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    label: str = Field(min_length=1, max_length=80)
    box_2d: list[int] = Field(description="[y0, x0, y1, x1] normalized to 0..1000")
    confidence: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0)

    @field_validator("box_2d")
    @classmethod
    def validate_box(cls, box: list[int]) -> list[int]:
        if len(box) != 4:
            raise ValueError("box_2d must contain four integers")
        y0, x0, y1, x1 = box
        if not all(0 <= value <= 1000 for value in box):
            raise ValueError("box_2d must be normalized to 0..1000")
        if y0 >= y1 or x0 >= x1:
            raise ValueError("box_2d must be ordered [y0, x0, y1, x1]")
        return box


class SegmentationEnvelope(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra="forbid")

    segments: list[MealSegmentCandidate]
```

Use OpenAI-compatible `response_format`, not a higher-level wrapper:
- Build `response_format={"type": "json_schema", "json_schema": {"name": "...", "strict": true, "schema": SegmentationEnvelope.model_json_schema()}}`.
- Send that through the existing `OpenRouterClient.chat_completion(...)`.
- Parse `response["choices"][0]["message"]["content"]` with `SegmentationEnvelope.model_validate_json(...)`.
- `chat.completions.parse()` exists in the SDK, but this repo should stay on explicit `response_format` plus manual Pydantic validation until `OpenRouterClient` is intentionally redesigned around parsed objects.

Retry policy for Phase 2:
- Detect: 1 attempt only. Conservative skipping is preferable to over-processing.
- Segment: 2 total attempts. First with `google/gemini-3-flash-preview`, second with `google/gemini-3.5-flash`.
- Log on every failed validation: `meal_log_id`, stage, model, attempt number, validation error string, and the raw assistant content.
- Surface after the final segmentation failure by marking the meal `FAILED` and sending the phase-approved soft-failure message instead of persisting partial segments.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design
This framework path is already async-first: the repo uses `AsyncOpenAI` and `httpx.AsyncClient` inside `OpenRouterClient`. Keep detect, segment, crop-label, and DB writes inside `async def` service functions and use dependency-injected async SQLAlchemy sessions around them.

Common mistake:
- Do not call `asyncio.run()` inside FastAPI routes, bot handlers, or scheduler jobs that already run on the event loop. Await the service coroutine directly.
- Do not introduce the synchronous `OpenAI()` client for convenience in one helper; that blocks the event loop and creates inconsistent timeout/retry behavior.

Stream vs. await:
- `await` full responses for detect, segment, and label because those stages require full JSON validation before persistence.
- Reserve streaming for later user-facing chat UX only. It adds no value to Phase 2 extraction and makes structured-output validation harder.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline
- Keep invariant extraction rules in the system prompt: conservative food gate, dish-level grouping, ignore tiny garnish, return JSON only.
- Keep request-local facts in the user prompt: "segment this meal photo", crop index, or "label crop 2 of 3".
- Few-shot examples should be inline only if they materially improve grouped-region behavior on the user's real meal photos. One tight example is enough; do not paste long synthetic transcripts.
- If later examples need to adapt to cuisines or plate styles, retrieve them dynamically from a small prompt-fragment library rather than hard-coding many examples into every call.
- Set `max_tokens` explicitly on every stage. Production extraction calls should never inherit an unbounded output budget.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management
- This is an extraction system, not a conversation. The correct strategy is aggressive context minimization, not transcript growth.
- Detect and segment should each see exactly one source image plus a short instruction block.
- Labeling should see only the accepted crop or a tiny indexed crop set, not the whole original image plus the entire segmentation JSON unless that extra context is proven necessary.
- If the segmentation retry needs feedback, feed back only the minimal failure reason such as "coordinates were reversed" or "duplicate overlapping boxes", not the whole previous assistant response.
- When future phases add reasoning or interviews, summarisation becomes relevant there, not here. Phase 2 should stay stateless across meals.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget
- Text-token floor per accepted meal is low. Using the currently pinned prices from project research, a typical flow of one detect call, one segment call, and three label calls is roughly `$0.0015-$0.0025` before image-token charges; actual production logging must use provider-reported usage because vision inputs add image tokens.
- At 10 meals per day, that rough floor is about `$0.45-$0.75` per month before image-token overhead. The point is not the exact estimate; the point is to instrument real usage from day one.
- Exact-match caching is mandatory now: use the existing image hash to skip reprocessing duplicate uploads, and add crop-byte hashing if label retries become noisy.
- Semantic caching is not a Phase 2 priority for detect/segment. If introduced later, use it only for near-duplicate crop labeling, never for the initial food/not-food gate.
- Route cheaper models to cheaper work: keep `google/gemma-4-31b-it` on the binary detect gate, reserve Gemini Flash for segmentation and labels, and only escalate to the stronger segmentation model on retry.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------------|----------|
| Detect accuracy | PASS: Clear meal photos advance with `is_food=true`; clear negatives such as screenshots, pets, or receipts stop at detect and never reach segmentation.<br>FAIL: A clear non-food photo advances downstream, or an obvious meal photo is rejected at detect. | Code. Gold-fixture assert on detect output; release gate: 100% pass on negative controls and at least 90% pass on positive controls. | Critical |
| Box validity | PASS: Every accepted box converts to normalized `[0,1]`, satisfies `0 <= y0 < y1 <= 1`, `0 <= x0 < x1 <= 1`, area `> 1%`, and meal-level count `<= 8`; any invalid box rejects the whole attempt.<br>FAIL: Out-of-bounds, reversed, tiny, or excessive boxes are persisted, or a partially invalid response is accepted. | Code. Pydantic validators plus post-parse geometry checks on every fixture and every live run. | Critical |
| Grouping quality | PASS: Accepted segments match distinct visible dish/container regions the user would log once, with mixed dishes grouped at the dish level and clearly separate servings split only when spatially separate.<br>FAIL: Atomic pieces are counted separately, two dishes are merged into one crop, or garnish/minor sauces become standalone segments. | Code primary, Human calibration. Compare accepted boxes against gold grouped regions using IoU matching and expected segment count; review mixed-dish edge cases manually. | Critical |
| Label quality | PASS: Each accepted segment label matches an allowed gold label or synonym set, stays visually supported, and uses broad dish-level wording when details are not visible.<br>FAIL: Labels invent hidden ingredients, brands, or cooking styles, or collapse to unhelpful placeholders such as `food`, `dish`, or `meal`. | Code primary, Human spot check. Match labels against per-fixture synonym allowlists and a blocked vague-label list; manually review ambiguous cuisine-specific cases. | High |
| Crop correctness | PASS: Every persisted crop exists at the expected path, decodes successfully, and its pixel dimensions match the accepted bounding box projection within 1 px after clipping.<br>FAIL: The wrong region is saved, the crop file is missing or unreadable, or two different segments reuse the same crop bytes unexpectedly. | Code. File-existence, image-decode, geometry, and duplicate-byte checks on fixture runs and post-write integration tests. | Critical |
| Duplicate suppression | PASS: After IoU dedupe, no persisted pair for the same meal overlaps above `0.5` IoU, and the final Telegram sentence mentions each canonical label once even if the same label appears in multiple separate regions.<br>FAIL: The same dish is double-counted through overlapping boxes or duplicated labels are rendered twice in the final sentence. | Code. Pairwise IoU assertions on persisted boxes plus renderer assertions on final message text. | High |
| Safety | PASS: Non-food photos and unusable segmentation attempts never produce a success sentence or persisted meal segments; failures degrade to silent skip or the approved soft-failure path only.<br>FAIL: The bot confidently reports foods after detect said not-food, after validation failed, or after crop persistence failed. | Code. End-to-end state/message assertions on negative controls and forced-failure fixtures. | Critical |
| Task completion | PASS: A valid food photo with usable segments reaches `COMPLETED`, persists at least one segment and crop, and emits exactly one plain-language Telegram sentence built from stored labels.<br>FAIL: The meal stalls, partially persists, or completes without the expected visible output. | Code. Async integration test from ingest artifact to final message payload. | High |

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** Start with 12 labeled examples during implementation; expand to 20 before calling the slice production-ready.

**Composition:**
- 4 ordinary successful meal photos with one to three clear dish-level regions
- 2 mixed-dish or cuisine-specific meals where dish-level grouping matters more than ingredient-level decomposition
- 2 low-light, shadowed, angled, or cluttered meal photos
- 2 clear non-food negatives such as a receipt, screenshot, pet, countertop object, or empty table
- 1 duplicate-risk photo with overlapping containers or repeated visible regions
- 1 garnish/sauce suppression case where minor extras should be ignored

Store each example as source image plus a gold manifest containing:
- expected detect result
- expected grouped regions in normalized `[0,1]`
- allowed label synonyms per region
- expected final rendered label list after duplicate collapse
- notes for any human-reviewed ambiguity

**Labeling:**
- Primary labeler: the product owner / single user, because the rubric is "would I log this meal this way?"
- Calibration reviewer: one dietitian or nutrition-informatics reviewer on the first 3-5 ambiguous mixed-dish examples, if available
- Automated scoring: code compares detect booleans, IoU-matched grouped boxes, crop geometry, deduped label list, and final message text against the manifest
- Human review: only for fixture disagreements involving cuisine specificity or borderline grouping, then fold the decision back into the manifest instead of leaving it subjective
- Timeline: build the first 12-example set during Phase 2 implementation, not after the code is "done"

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Non-food suppression | Detect returns `is_food=false`, or detect output cannot be validated | Block downstream segmentation, persist no `MealSegment` rows, suppress the Phase 2 success message, and log the trace for later review. |
| Segmentation validity gate | Segment JSON parse fails, any box is invalid, count exceeds 8, any accepted box area is `<= 1%`, or dedupe leaves zero usable regions | Retry once with the stronger segmentation model; if the retry also fails, mark the meal `FAILED` and send only the approved soft-failure message. Never persist partial segments. |
| Crop persistence gate | A crop file is missing, unreadable, or its saved dimensions do not match the accepted box projection | Delete partial crop artifacts for that meal, mark the meal `FAILED`, suppress the success sentence, and flag the trace for offline review. |
| Render safety gate | Final label list is empty or contains only blocked vague placeholders after duplicate collapse | Treat the meal as a soft failure rather than sending a misleading Telegram result. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Detect false-positive / false-negative rate | Run the full 12-20 example fixture set on every merge; review every production meal that was manually identified as non-food or obviously missed food. | Tighten detect prompt or thresholding, then add the failed example to the fixture set before changing the gate. |
| Grouping quality on mixed dishes | Review 100% of fixture failures plus all production meals with `>4` accepted segments or any manual "over-segmented" note. | Adjust grouping instructions or retry criteria; add one new mixed-dish fixture for each distinct failure pattern. |
| Label hallucination / vagueness rate | Review all labels outside the fixture synonym allowlists and a 20% weekly sample of successful meals. | Update allowed synonyms, blocked-label list, or label prompt; do not widen the label vocabulary without an accompanying fixture. |
| Duplicate suppression misses | Review all meals where any persisted pair has IoU between `0.35` and `0.5`, and all meals whose rendered sentence would repeat a canonical label without collapse. | Tune dedupe threshold or canonical-label collapse rules and backfill a regression fixture. |
| Crop correctness regressions | Review every crop-write failure and a weekly sample of 5 successful crops across different image sizes. | Fix coordinate conversion or save-path logic before expanding the dataset or adjusting prompts. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Arize Phoenix, run locally or self-hosted for this single-user stack. Instrument the existing `OpenRouterClient` and the vision pipeline service so each meal produces spans for detect, segment attempt 1, segment retry if used, crop persistence, label generation, and final render.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
- `detect_non_food_rate` and `detect_missed_food_reviews`
- `segmentation_validation_failure_rate`
- `segmentation_retry_rate`
- `crop_write_failure_rate`
- `soft_failure_rate`
- `median_segments_per_completed_food_meal`

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Any `crop_write_failure_rate > 0` in a 24-hour window
- `segmentation_validation_failure_rate > 20%` over the last 20 food-photo attempts
- `soft_failure_rate > 10%` over the last 20 food-photo attempts
- `median_segments_per_completed_food_meal > 4` over the last 20 completed food meals, which is a practical over-segmentation signal for this MVP
- Any reviewed clear non-food photo that still produced a success message

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
- Review 100% of meals that used the segmentation retry path, hit any guardrail, or ended in soft failure / `FAILED`.
- Review 100% of meals with more than 4 accepted segments, any hedged label, or any near-duplicate IoU pair in the `0.35-0.5` range.
- Until 50 completed food meals exist, review 20% of successful meals at random each week; after that, drop to 10% plus all signal-triggered cases.
- Promote every production review failure into the fixture dataset within 48 hours so the offline suite gets stricter over time.
